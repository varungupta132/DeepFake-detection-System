Under the Hood

The Technology

A deep dive into the multi-signal fusion architecture that powers DeepScan's detection engine.

Detection Architecture

DeepScan AI combines a fine-tuned deep neural network with six independent computer vision signal analyzers. Each signal captures a different dimension of video authenticity — from pixel-level compression artifacts to high-level temporal motion patterns. The outputs are fused into a single confidence score and verdict.

This hybrid approach outperforms single-model detectors because deepfake generation methods vary widely. A GAN-based face swap may fool a temporal analyzer but leave compression artifacts; a diffusion-based synthesis may pass artifact checks but fail face quality scoring. Fusion catches what any single signal misses.

TensorFlow MobileNetV2 Core

The primary classifier is built on MobileNetV2, a lightweight convolutional architecture optimized for efficient inference. The model is fine-tuned on a curated dataset of real and synthetically generated video frames, learning to distinguish authentic facial textures from AI-generated ones.

Key advantages of MobileNetV2 for this task:

  • Inverted residual blocks preserve gradient flow through deep layers
  • Depthwise separable convolutions reduce compute without sacrificing accuracy
  • Linear bottlenecks prevent information loss in low-dimensional feature spaces
  • Fast inference enables real-time frame-by-frame analysis

6-Signal Computer Vision Fusion

Each of the six signals is computed independently and contributes to the final suspicion index.

Temporal Consistency

Measures frame-to-frame coherence. Deepfakes often introduce subtle flickering or discontinuities between frames that are invisible to the naked eye but detectable statistically.

Compression Artifacts

Analyzes DCT block boundaries and quantization noise patterns. AI-generated content frequently exhibits atypical compression signatures when re-encoded.

Face Quality

Scores facial region sharpness, symmetry, and landmark consistency. Synthesized faces often have subtle asymmetries or blurring at the hairline and ear boundaries.

Edge Consistency

Examines gradient transitions at face-background boundaries. Blending artifacts from face-swap operations leave characteristic edge signatures.

Color Distribution

Compares color histograms and skin tone distributions across frames. Deepfake generators sometimes produce subtle color shifts or inconsistent lighting.

Motion Naturalness

Evaluates optical flow patterns against models of natural human motion. Synthetic videos can exhibit unnatural micro-movements or overly smooth motion trajectories.

Processing Pipeline

When a video is submitted, the backend executes the following pipeline:

  • Frame Extraction: Intelligent sampling selects representative frames, balancing coverage and compute cost.
  • Face Detection: A face detector isolates facial regions for targeted analysis.
  • Parallel Signal Computation: All six CV signals are computed concurrently across extracted frames.
  • Neural Inference: MobileNetV2 runs on each frame and scores are aggregated.
  • Score Fusion: Signal scores and neural outputs are combined using a weighted fusion model.
  • Verdict Generation: The fused score is thresholded to produce a REAL/FAKE verdict with confidence.
